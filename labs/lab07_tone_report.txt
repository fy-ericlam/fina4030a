4. HOW MUCH OF THAT SURVIVES? (this is the part that matters)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  (a) Persistence. tone_z lag-1 autocorr = 0.77 pooled, 0.79 statements-only.
      Effective independent observations: ~24 of 187 pooled, ~11 of 95 statements.
      The Fed changes stance a few times a decade; we have ~4 regimes, not
      187 experiments. Every p-value above assumes independence and is
      therefore far too generous.

  (b) Correct null. At h=21, hit 0.598:
        naive binomial (assumes independence)  p = 0.0097
        iid permutation (also assumes it)      p = 0.0058
        circular shift (preserves persistence) p = 0.1126   <-- believe this one
      Once the signal's own persistence is respected, the edge is not
      distinguishable from chance.

  (c) Subsample stability:
        2015-2019    n= 79  hit=0.570  edge=+0.070
        2020-2026    n=105  hit=0.619  edge=+0.119
        first half   n= 93  hit=0.581  edge=+0.081
        second half  n= 91  hit=0.615  edge=+0.115
        statements   n= 94  hit=0.628  edge=+0.128
        minutes      n= 90  hit=0.567  edge=+0.067
      The edge is roughly 1.4x larger in the second half of the
      sample than the first (+0.115 vs +0.081), and +0.128 on statements
      vs +0.067 on minutes. It is not a stable effect across
      periods or document types.

  (d) Jackknife -- drop one calendar year at a time:
        2015:+0.12  2016:+0.11  2017:+0.09  2018:+0.10  2019:+0.08  2020:+0.08
        2021:+0.11  2022:+0.10  2023:+0.11  2024:+0.10  2025:+0.07  2026:+0.11
      edge range +0.069 to +0.121 -- no single year
      drives it, so it is a broad weak regime effect rather than one
      lucky episode. That is the one point in the signal's favour.

  (e) Multiple testing: 2 signals x 5 horizons = 10 tests. The headline
      p-value is not adjusted for that; at h=1 the same rule is
      significantly WRONG, which is what noise mining looks like.

  (f) Walk-forward: choose the signal/horizon on the first half of the
      sample only, then apply that choice to the second half untouched.
      picked in-sample: tone_z h=21  ->  train hit 0.581 (edge +0.081, n=93)
      applied out-of-sample:            test  hit 0.615 (edge +0.115, n=91, p=0.035)
      This one comes out IN FAVOUR of the signal and is the strongest
      evidence for it. Weigh it against (g).

  (g) Early era (through 2018-11) vs the rest, every cell:
        tone_z      h1:-0.12 h3:-0.06 h5:-0.05 h10:-0.04 h21:+0.02
        tone_z_chg  h1:-0.15 h3:-0.10 h5:+0.00 h10:-0.00 h21:-0.03
      8 of 10 cells are NEGATIVE in the early era. The relationship
      does not merely weaken before ~2019 -- it points the other way. A
      desk that committed to this signal early would have been paid to
      hold the opposite position. Combined with (f), what this really
      shows is one sign flip, not a stable law.

6. THE TRAP WE AVOIDED (why release_date matters)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  minutes timed on meeting_date   h=10: hit 0.625  h=21: hit 0.576
  minutes timed on release_date   h=10: hit 0.544  h=21: hit 0.567
  Back-dating minutes to the meeting inflates the hit rate purely by
  using text that was still 3 weeks from publication. Anyone reporting a
  strong result off this dataset has probably done exactly that.

==============================================================================
VERDICT
==============================================================================
  FOR:     the index is well built -- statements and minutes on the same
           meeting agree at r=0.78, the history matches the actual cycle,
           no single year drives the result, and the walk-forward split
           held up out of sample.
  AGAINST: no same-day reaction at all; the edge exists only at 10-21
           sessions; ~24 effective observations, not 187; the honest
           null gives p~0.11, not 0.01; and the sign is inverted in the
           first four years of the sample.

  The binding constraint is economics, not statistics. Even taking the
  hit rate at face value, the signal is worth ~1.2 bp per release against
  a 15.7 bp standard deviation -- an annualised IR near 0.3 gross, which
  a 2s10s spread trade will not clear after cost. You would be betting
  the 2019+ sign is the true one on ~24 independent observations.

  RECOMMENDATION: do not build a standalone timing signal on this. Keep
  the index as a stance descriptor / conditioning variable, where its
  genuine construct validity is worth something and no directional bet
  rides on the weak part. If it is taken further, the test to run is
  whether tone adds anything beyond the OIS-implied path already priced
  -- the missing same-day reaction suggests it will not.
